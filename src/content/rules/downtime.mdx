An adventure isn’t truly complete without all the exciting aspects that make up a full story. Some Characters might be expert craftsmen, skilled in animal companionship, or simply looking for a place to call home. Downtime introduces variety to gameplay and helps flesh out what Characters do between their epic exploits.

While crafting is already covered in detail in the previous section, this chapter introduces other downtime pursuits such as recruiting Companions, training skills and proficiencies, gathering valuable information, establishing and upgrading a home base, and preparing for future quests. These activities provide flavor, world-building, and long-term progression opportunities for players who wish to explore them.

Downtime is intended to enrich the game experience for those who engage with it, but not every player may find it appealing. For that reason, it’s a “get what you put in” system; entirely optional and tailored to each player's level of interest.

Time Requirement. By default, downtime is measured in 5-day increments. However, depending on the pace and tone of the campaign, the Realm Master may adjust this duration to suit the story. Downtime segments can be shortened to 3-day or even 1-day intervals to match the campaign’s flow and player needs.

Group Downtime. Party members may help each other with any downtime activity that gives an external reward. For example, a player looking to use the Criminal Activity downtime option to perform a heist can get their party involved, whereas a player looking to use the training downtime option to gain a Skill Point for themselves must make that effort on their own. When attempting to help in downtime activities, such as Study or Socialize, you may use the same rules outlined in the Helping section of Chapter 1: Playing the Game.

For downtime activities that require multiple skill rolls, you may treat them as a skill encounter with everyone involved, increasing the required successes by 1 for each additional participant.

<h2 id="bonding-training-companions">Bonding & Training Companions</h2>

A Companion is any Non-Playable Creature or Character (NPC) that you recruit to work alongside you, following your instructions learned through earned trust, training, and mutual respect.

Companions can take many forms, including tamed wild animals, created constructs, allied Characters, summoned creatures, and so on.

It’s up to your Realm Master what type of creatures you are able to form a bond with, and they may require you to make additional Skill rolls or even participate in a Skill Encounter before you’re able to begin bonding a creature.

<h3 id="general-bonding-guidelines">General Bonding Guidelines</h3>

Bonding requires 3 main things: Time, Skill, and Currency.

- Time. The time required to Bond with a creature is equal to the creature’s level multiplied by 5 in days. Every 5 days, you will make a Bonding Skill roll as outlined in the next section, with the total at the end of the bonding period needing to be equal to or greater than the required number of successes to bond.

- Skill. You must succeed a number of Bonding Skill rolls equal to the creature’s level times 2. Each Success adds to the total required, and each Failure reduces the Successes obtained.

The Bonding Skill roll Difficulty Score is equal to the creature’s level multiplied by 2 + 10.

- Currency. The currency cost to bond with a creature represents different supplies to shelter, feed, construct, or otherwise care for the creature during the bonding period. The cost of currency is paid throughout the entire bonding process. Currency cost is represented on the creature bonding table and is adjusted by level.

After successfully completing the bonding process, the creature is considered your Companion and can interact in roleplay, Skill, and Combat Encounters as outlined in Chapter 2: Encounters.

Breaking up Bonding Periods

A bonding period does not need to occur all at once. Instead, it can be done over multiple different periods, provided the total number of time, currency, and  Successes required are all met by the end of the total time.

Bonding Skill Encounters

Although bonding with a creature is not typically a group effort, in some situations, a group can collaborate to help bond a single creature. In these cases, Characters assisting with the bonding who have the relevant skill can make a Bonding Skill roll every 5 days they participate. For each Success beyond the first, they can reduce the time required by 5 days per 5-day period.

<h3 id="training-companions">Training Companions</h3>

Similarly to how you Bond a Companion, you can increase a Companion's level up to a maximum of half of your level through training that companion, which requires a similar concerted effort with Currency, Time, and Skill. The method for Training a Companion follows a pattern of comparing the current level of the creature to the next level, requiring spending the difference between the two in cost and time as described below.

Cost to Train. The cost to train a Companion from a lower level to a higher one, in materials or currency, is based on the difference between the Companion's current level and the next level on the Bonding Table.

To calculate, subtract the current level's Currency to bond from the next one; this is the material cost to increase a level. For example, if the current level was 2, then the next level is 3, so the training would require the difference between the two, 700 for level 3 bonding, minus 400 for a level 2 bonding, giving a cost of 300.

Time Requirement. The time required to train a Companion is determined by the difference in bonding times listed on the Bonding Table, based on the current level versus the next level. For example, if you’re training a Companion level 1/2 to level 3/4, and the table shows 3 days for the current level, and 4 days for the next one, the training would take 4 - 3 = 1 day.

Difficulty Score & Successes. The Difficulty Score for training a Companion is the same as the Difficulty Score of the higher level you desire to train to, while the number of required Successes is the difference between the Successes needed for the current level and the next one. For example, if you’re training a Companion of level 2 (requiring 4 Successes) to level 3 (requiring 6 Successes), the difference is 6 - 4 = 2 Successes needed.

The Skill used for Training is the same as that would be used for Bonding, as shown in the Bonding Skills above.

<h2 id="training-skills-defenses">Training Skills & Defenses</h2>

With time and persistent effort, you can gain and improve Skills and Defenses using Training Points through a process called Training. The process of gaining proficiency can take many forms and may require finding a suitable trainer, environment, or resources before beginning. At the Realm Master's discretion, this process can be mediated through roleplay or Skill Encounters.

When gaining Skills through training, you do so by earning Skill Points in exchange and spending them on the desired Skill. This method also applies to Defenses, but increasing a Defense requires more resources, as each Defense increase costs 2 Skill Points.

<h3 id="training-guidelines">Training Guidelines</h3>

Training requires an investment of Training Points, Time, Skill, and Currency.

- Training Points. The Training Points required to gain a Skill Point with Downtime is equal to 6 Training Points per Skill Point. If you wish to increase a defense by 1 instead, this requires 2 Skill Points, meaning the cost is 12 Training Points.

- Time. The time required to train is equal to 5 days per 2 Training Points required.

- Skill. A Training Skill Roll uses either the Skill or Defense being trained, even if non-instinctive.

You must succeed a number of Training Skill rolls equal to the Training Point Requirement. Each rolled Success adds to the total Successes obtained, and each Failure reduces the total successes obtained.

The Training Skill roll Difficulty Score is equal to the Training Points Required + 10.

- Currency. The currency cost to train is equal to 75 per Training Point required.

<h3 id="criminal-activity-guidelines">Criminal Activity Guidelines</h3>

Calling on your criminal connections can mean bringing another professional onto the job or assembling a team for a heist. <h2 id="arena-combat">Arena Combat</h2>

In most cities, you can find work entertaining the masses with feats of combat prowess. Whether by brutal efficiency or flashy moves, there is good money to be had in putting your body and pride on the line to entertain.

<h3 id="arena-combat-guidelines">Arena Combat Guidelines</h3>

Fighting in the arena will test your combat prowess and ability to work a crowd. Your success and popularity will determine your earnings.

- Choose a Weapon. The weapon, or Innate power, you choose will determine which ability you use for the fight. Additionally, your weapon or innate power will give bonuses based on its damage dice and properties.

- Damage Dice. You will gain a bonus to your attack roll equal to half the TP cost of the damage. This includes TP spent gaining and increasing Unarmed Prowess. For innate powers, this includes Long-Linger damage and true damage.

- Properties & Parts. You will gain a bonus to your Perform or Entertain skill roll equal to half the TP cost of your weapon’s properties. If you are using Unarmed Prowess, you instead gain a bonus equal to the skill points you’ve spent in Acrobatics or Athletics. If you are using an innate power, this bonus is calculated with the TP cost of non-damaging parts in your power.

- Choose Difficulty. Choose a difficulty from the Arena Difficulty and Crowd Engagement table below.

- Determine Combat Prowess. Make an attack roll using the ability of your chosen weapon or Innate power, adding the bonuses outlined in the Damage Dice section above.

- Determine Crowd Appeal. Make a Perform or Entertain skill roll, adding the bonuses outlined in the Properties & Parts section above.

- Gain Popularity. After each combat, you will add 1 + any additional Successes to your Popularity Rank. As you increase your Popularity rank, your earnings will likewise increase as bets on you flow and you’re allowed into more prestigious arenas. How your Popularity Rank affects your earnings is shown in the Popularity table below.

Using your skills, you may work in the community of whatever settlement you happen to find yourself in. <h2 id="study">Study</h2>

A strong knowledge base is essential for any adventurer looking to achieve greatness! Whether you’re a bookish technician looking to expand your pool of knowledge or a sensible knight honing your fighting style and learning the weaknesses of your enemies, spending time in a library or with a mentor can help you immensely.

<h3 id="fragments-of-wisdom">Fragments of Wisdom</h3>

After exhaustive study on a very specific subject, you gain a fleeting insight into deeper truths. The rewards for this course of study are called Fragments of Wisdom. These are stored Successes with the ability you choose to use below, and can be used when you make a check using that skill to enhance a success or mitigate a failure.

- Choose a Skill. This skill must reflect what you would like to study as it will be used in and gain the benefits from this activity.

- Roll. Using the skill that you chose, using either Intelligence or Acuity instead of the skill’s usual ability. The DS for this check is 12 + 2 for each Fragment you already have from this downtime activity. For each Success, gain 1 Fragment for the chosen skill.

- Take Notes. Possibly the most important step in every downtime activity, but crucial for this one. Carefully record which skills you gained Fragments for, and how many in each skill.

<h3 id="guided-study">Guided Study</h3>

Working with a mentor, you may hone or refocus your talents and aptitudes. Mentors usually charge for their services, with a typical price being 75c for the workweek. However, your RM may offer opportunities for a mentor as quest rewards or story devices.

Note: You must have access to a mentor for this downtime activity.

- Choose a Focus. This focus can be a weapon part, armor part, power part, or technique part.

- Determine Bonuses. For weapon and technique parts, use their relevant ability + your martial archetype bonus. For armor parts, use Vitality + your martial archetype bonus unless the armor has an ability requirement; then, you may use that ability. For power parts, use your power ability + power archetype bonus.

- Roll. The DS for this check is 15 + 2 x the TP cost of your chosen focus. On a success, you gain proficiency in your chosen focus. Alternatively, you may choose to lose proficiency in other areas and refocus the TP into your chosen focus without a Skill Rolls.

<h2 id="downtime-skill-encounters">Downtime Skill Encounters</h2>

There is an abundance of activities Characters can take up during their downtime.
